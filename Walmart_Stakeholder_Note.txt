Based on the data, about 12.7% of store-weeks had strong sales, meaning sales were at least 10% higher than the store's average. Thanksgiving had the highest chance of strong sales at 82%, while November and December had a 41% chance compared to 8.5% during other months.
The holiday-only staffing rule was correct 27% of the time but missed 85% of strong weeks, while the November–December rule performed better with 41% precision and recall. Our Naive Bayes model did not perform better than the holiday rule because it failed to predict any strong 
weeks in the 2012 test data, even though it had about 87% accuracy. One limitation is that the 2012 data does not include November or December, so I think we should be careful about relying on the model and focus more on preparing extra staff for Thanksgiving and the holiday season.
